## 1. Retention of documentation is proportional to the value of the subject
_2026-07-28_

**Maxim.** How much record a thing deserves scales with what the thing is worth —
its durability, risk, reach, and consequence. Provisional, held, or low-stakes work
gets thin documentation; foundational, high-risk, widely-consumed work earns depth.

**Why.** Over-documenting a cheap subject is itself a defect: it buries the
signal, taxes every future review, and misrepresents importance by sheer volume.
Under-documenting a valuable subject loses hard-won knowledge. The skill is
matching weight to worth, not maximizing either direction.

**Kin.** Same family as the **Simplification Rule** and **Representative by
Design** (`AI_PORTAL.md`) and the **Scope-Calibrated Lifecycle** doctrine — apply
the depth the case justifies, no more.

**Origin.** Caught while reviewing an external-AI (Grok) design intake: the local
workbench produced six files for a proposal that was still *held pending
corrections and a priority call* — heavier than the subject warranted at that
stage. The record should have been the required evidence plus one thin note.

**Teaching note.** A lesson on documentation economy / signal-to-noise: give
students a mix of subjects (a throwaway spike, a shared library, a security
boundary) and have them defend the right documentation weight for each. Pair with
scope-calibrated lifecycle — teach the whole lifecycle, spend only the stages the
case earns.
