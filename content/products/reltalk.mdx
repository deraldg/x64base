## Overview

RelTalk names the relation-facing workflow in the DotTalk++ / x64base system.

Its subject is connected data across work areas: declared relation edges,
asymmetric same-field/cross-owner links, saved workspace relation state, and
rooted traversal through `ERSATZ`.

The local project includes same-field relation support and asymmetric relation forms such as:

```text
REL ADD SYSCMD SYSSUBCMD ON CAN_NAME TO PARENT
REL ADD SYSSUBCMD SYSENTVAR ON QUAL_NAME TO HELP_OWNR
```

Current relation-facing surfaces include:

- `REL ADD`
- `REL LIST ALL`
- `REL JOIN [DISTINCT|ALL] [LIMIT <n>] [<child> ...] TUPLE <alias>.<field>[, ...]`
- `REL ENUM [LIMIT <n>] [<child> ...] TUPLE <alias>.<field>[, ...]`
- `REL REFRESH`
- `REL SCANLIMIT [<n>]`
- `WORKSPACE SAVE` / `WORKSPACE LOAD`
- `ERSATZ`, `ERSATZ PATH`, and `RBROWSE`
- workspace graph and GUI contract docs

## The join

`REL JOIN` walks a declared parent and its children and emits one tuple per
combination, projecting the `<alias>.<field>` list named after `TUPLE`. It is a true
enumerator: it delegates to the same walker as `REL ENUM`
(`src/cli/join_engine.cpp`). The historical single-row form -- one tuple built
from wherever the relation pointers currently sit -- is preserved as
`REL JOIN ONE`, and `ONE` given a child chain is **refused rather than
silently ignored**, because a traversal that never happened should not report
success (AIF-147).

`REL SCANLIMIT` sets the relation engine's per-hop record budget. It caps what a
traversal **finds**, not what is displayed -- lowering it changes match counts
and drops join rows, where `ERSATZ LIMIT` only shortens the screen. A truncated
`REL JOIN` or `REL ENUM` restates the limit and the row count at the result, and
`REL LIST` says it is reporting lower bounds, because a truncated count is not a
short list: it is a wrong number in the shape of a right one.
