Use this page to evaluate the Laboratory Campus without first traversing the
complete technical documentation set. The pathway links the research question,
one guided lesson, runtime evidence, research safeguards, and collaboration
entry points.

## A 30-Minute Evaluation Path

1. Read the [Academic Positioning and Research Agenda](/docs/labtalk/academic-positioning)
   to understand the candidate research contribution and its claim boundary.
2. Review the guided exemplar,
   [Records, Fields, and Tables](/docs/labtalk/lesson-records-fields-tables),
   to see how a concept, command surface, dataset, observation, and proof record
   become a lesson.
3. Inspect the [Runtime Evidence Gallery](/docs/labtalk/runtime-evidence) to
   distinguish implemented system behavior from educational hypotheses.
4. Review the [evidence and research safeguards](/docs/labtalk/academic-positioning#evidence-and-research-safeguards)
   before proposing classroom evaluation or human-subjects research.
5. Use [Suggest a Lesson](/docs/labtalk/suggest-a-lesson) to offer curriculum
   review, a dataset, a lesson idea, or a research question.

## Central Academic Question

The Laboratory Campus asks whether students can learn database systems more
deeply when commands, data, runtime state, documentation, provenance, and proof
remain inspectable in one environment.

The current site presents that proposition as a research direction. It does not
claim measured learning gains, established novelty, a completed curriculum, or
formal institutional adoption.

## What Can Be Evaluated Now

- the x64base and DotTalk++ runtime surfaces documented by source and runtime
  evidence;
- the proof-aware lesson model;
- the separation of implementation evidence from learning evidence;
- the suitability of the guided lesson structure for introductory database
  education;
- the proposed research questions, safeguards, and collaboration boundaries.

## What Still Needs Academic Work

- a reviewed primary-source bibliography;
- instructor review of lesson sequencing and assessment;
- accessibility and prerequisite review;
- controlled classroom or usability studies;
- appropriate consent and institutional review for human-subjects research;
- publication of limitations, failed hypotheses, and negative results.

## Collaboration Paths

The project is seeking critique, not endorsement by implication. Useful review
can come from database educators, computing education researchers, learning
scientists, HCI researchers, programming-language researchers, technical
writing educators, students, and general-education specialists.

Start with [Suggest a Lesson](/docs/labtalk/suggest-a-lesson), the
[Contribution Guide](/docs/dev/contribution-guide), or the
[Academic Positioning and Research Agenda](/docs/labtalk/academic-positioning).
