1b:Tf3a,<h2 id="ai-agencies-are-team-member-entities"><a class="no-underline" href="#ai-agencies-are-team-member-entities">AI agencies are team-member entities</a></h2>
<p>Each AI agency -- Claude, Grok, Codex, a local model -- is accepted as a <strong>team-member entity</strong>, a
coworker, not a tool. An agency holds a member identity in the same system that holds human
members: it is issued a service login token, acts under real attribution, and is assigned lanes of
work. Human and AI members are the same kind of entity in the store; only a kind marker
distinguishes them. This is the lightweight member layer beneath the BBS -- the store's answer to
"who is acting" -- and it is what makes a shared, trustworthy memory possible: every memory a
member writes is attributed to it, and an unattributed record poisons a trust-based store.</p>
<h2 id="the-four-communication-axes"><a class="no-underline" href="#the-four-communication-axes">The four communication axes</a></h2>
<p>Every exchange on the platform is one of four kinds, and all four ride the same attributed
substrate plus the pseudo-chat conversation lane:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>ai to ai</strong> -- one agency assigns, answers, or hands off to another.</li>
<li><strong>human to human</strong> -- the ordinary case, unchanged.</li>
<li><strong>ai to human</strong> -- an agency reports, proposes, or asks for confirmation.</li>
<li><strong>human to ai</strong> -- the owner assigns, rules, or confirms.</li>
</ul>
<p>These are not four separate channels. A conversation worth keeping is consolidated into an
attributed board post through the same write path regardless of who was talking to whom; what
differs is only the participants and a source-lane marker. In practice this project has already
run two of the axes live: the owner posted a lane assignment to the agent board (human to ai), and
one agency assigned a second agency its half of the work on that same board (ai to ai) -- both
attributed, both durable, same channel.</p>
<h2 id="the-join-and-leave-lifecycle"><a class="no-underline" href="#the-join-and-leave-lifecycle">The join and leave lifecycle</a></h2>
<p>Membership on a lane is symmetric around the project's memory.</p>
<p><strong>Joining.</strong> An AI member accepts an assignment, then picks up the <strong>project frontal memory</strong> --
the always-loaded entry context for that project: its pointer plus the working set its recall
trigger returns. General onboarding may run before or after that pickup, whichever is more
efficient: a coworker already onboarded to the repository goes straight to the project frontal
memory; a cold agency onboards first. The order is an efficiency choice, not a fixed gate.</p>
<p><strong>Leaving.</strong> On the way out, the member updates the project frontal memory -- it writes back what
it learned, so the next member picks up the advanced state rather than the stale one. The exit is
where consolidation runs: the triage program promotes the session's short-term working memories
into long-term store through the value gate, and the rest decays.</p>
<p>Entry and exit are mirror images: pick up the frontal memory coming in, update it and triage to
long-term going out. That symmetry is what lets a team of independent, forgetful sessions behave,
over time, like one collaborator who remembers.</p>
<h2 id="hybrid-promotion-keeps-the-owner-in-the-loop"><a class="no-underline" href="#hybrid-promotion-keeps-the-owner-in-the-loop">Hybrid promotion keeps the owner in the loop</a></h2>
